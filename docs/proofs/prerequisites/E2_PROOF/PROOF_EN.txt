THEOREM: EXACT BILINEAR MIXED INTERACTION AT EXCESS TWO

Scope. This establishes the bilinear reconstruction formula only. It does
not establish the separate extremal/common-leaf-level maximum conjecture.
The proof uses the exact four-move transfer and the already established
V0 and V1 formulas. All variables below are integers or exact rationals.

Definitions.

Write P=D_0 ( D_1 ( ... ( D_h and Q=E_0 ( E_1 ( ... ( E_h.
Let u_i,v_i be the root counts. Let sigma(P) be the sum of (degree+1)^2
over all closed vertices. Set

 s(P)=sigma(P)-sum_i u_i,
 r_i(P)=sum of degrees of the roots of D_i,
 f(P)=(s(P),r_0(P),...,r_h(P)),
 K(u,v)=product_i binom(u_i+v_i,u_i),
 b_i=v_i/(u_i+1), a_i=u_i/(v_i+1).

For a closed tree T put alpha(T)=sigma(T)-1, beta(T)=degree(T).
Deleting T as an external root subtracts alpha(T) from s and beta(T)
from the root-degree sum at its level, regardless of T's size or shape.

Define H(u,v) by

 H_ss=K,
 H_{s,rQ_j}=K*a_j,
 H_{rP_i,s}=K*b_i,
 H_{rP_i,rQ_j}=K*b_i*a_j for i!=j,
 H_{rP_i,rQ_i}=K.

Then, for all prefixes P,Q of the given root profiles, there exist
one-sided functions A_{u,v}(P), B_{u,v}(Q) such that

                 V2(P,Q)=A_{u,v}(P)+B_{u,v}(Q)+f(P)^T H(u,v) f(Q).

The functions retain the full ordered prefixes. No claim that they are
determined by f is made. For any actual anchors P0,Q0 of these profiles,
subtracting four instances yields

 V2(P,Q)=V2(P,Q0)+V2(P0,Q)-V2(P0,Q0)
       +(f(P)-f(P0))^T H(u,v) (f(Q)-f(Q0)).

We prove the stronger first assertion, allowing different lengths within
a profile. V2 is always evaluated at a+b-h+4 for its own state.

LEMMA 1. AFFINE FORCING AND ROOT EXCURSIONS.

Let J=T1 V1+T2 V0 be the incoming positive-excess source for V2.
At fixed root profiles J is a sum of a function of P and a function of Q.
Moreover, in an internal deterministic excursion of a single external
root it is affine in the shape features of the unchanged opposite base.

Verification from the four moves is as follows. Mixed DD inverse images
are indexed by a chosen terminal root on either side. Their V1 values
are summed. The sum of their V0 coefficients is

 B=L*[binom(x+y,x+1)+binom(x+y,y+1)],
 L=product_{l<h} binom(u_l+v_l,u_l), x=u_h, y=v_h.

The resulting mixed-DD sum equals a profile-only constant plus

 B*(s(P)+s(Q))
 +sum_{l<h} B*[b_l*r_l(P)+a_l*r_l(Q)]
 +L*[binom(x+y,x+2)+binom(x+y,x)]*r_h(P)
 +L*[binom(x+y,y+2)+binom(x+y,x)]*r_h(Q)
 +L*sum_{k=0}^{x-1} binom(y+k,y)*degree(P-root k+1)
 +L*sum_{k=0}^{y-1} binom(x+k,x)*degree(Q-root k+1).

To check this identity directly, insert the known linear V1 formula in
each inverse image; all lower-level factors remain unchanged, the new
top level has an empty opposite forest, and the root index sums are
sum_{k=0}^{m-1} binom(c+k,c)=binom(c+m,c+1). The two last sums retain
the only ordered root-degree dependence. In particular there are no
products of a P shape feature and a Q shape feature.

The remaining T1 predecessors occur when the terminal forest on one
side is empty. They are exactly V1 for the opposite prefix closed by
one ')' and the empty terminal '(' removed on this side. Closing a
forest with c roots changes s by c(c+3), adds c to the preceding
root-degree sum, and increments its preceding root count. These are
profile-dependent constants in the affine V1 formula. Thus they too
are affine and separated. T2 V0 depends only on the profiles after
closing both final '(' and is shape independent.

Finally, reverse zero-excess moves act as follows. If the terminal
P forest has a last root T, remove its final ')' and append '(' to Q.
The state becomes (A '(' children(T),B '('), where A is P without T
and B=Q. Until this new level is completely erased the opposite top
forest is empty; hence the zero-excess reverse path is deterministic.
After the interior is erased, remove the two final '(' and return to
(A,B). A symmetric branch exists for a terminal Q root. If both
terminal forests are empty, only simultaneous removal of the two
final '(' is possible. Consequently reverse histories shuffle whole
root excursions at each level, preserving the order on each side.

LEMMA 2. EXACT OPPOSITE-SHAPE PART OF ONE EXCURSION.

Take a base A,B at common height i, with terminal root counts x,y and
K0=K(profile(A),profile(B)). Attach a P-side root T at level i.
Let C_T(A,B) be the sum of J along the internal reverse excursion,
starting at (A '(' children(T),B '(') and ending before (A,B).
Let C_leaf be the same quantity with T replaced by a single leaf.
The part of C_T-C_leaf depending on the shape of B is exactly

 K0 { [alpha(T)+beta(T)*y/(x+1)]
              [s(B)+sum_{l<i} u_l*r_l(B)/(v_l+1)]
       +[x*alpha(T)/(y+1)+beta(T)]*r_i(B) }.                 (1)

The difference between C_T-C_leaf and (1) is independent of B shape
at fixed base profiles. It can depend arbitrarily on T and A.

Proof with all coefficient corrections. Consider a state
(A '(' X,B '(') where the top forest X has c roots. Its extra level
has profiles c,0, so the mixed-DD coefficient B in Lemma 1 is K0*c.
The inverse T1 move closing the P side contributes V1(A '(' X ')',B).
Its V0 coefficient is K0*A0, where A0=(x+y+1)/(x+1).
When c=0 there is also the move closing Q, contributing V1(A,B '()'),
with V0 coefficient K0*B0, where B0=(x+y+1)/(y+1).
Thus the coefficient of s(B) in J/K0 is exactly

                         c+A0+1_{c=0} B0.                 (2)

For a lower r_l(B), l<i, all those predecessor profiles have unchanged
level l. Its coefficient is (2) times u_l/(v_l+1).
For r_i(B), closing P changes x to x+1 and closing Q changes y to y+1.
The coefficient in J/K0 is therefore exactly

 x*c/(y+1)+A0*(x+1)/(y+1)+1_{c=0} B0*x/(y+2).             (3)

The ordered-degree terms in Lemma 1 occur at the new top level and
contain no shape feature of B. T2 V0 has no such feature either.

Let S(T;x,y) be the coefficient of s(B) in C_T/K0. Write d=degree(T)
and let T_1,...,T_d be the ordered children of T. The outer-level
states have c=d,d-1,...,0. Each child T_k contributes its own internal
excursion with base profiles k-1,0 at the next level. Hence (2) gives

 S(T;x,y)=d(d+1)/2+(d+1)A0+B0+sum_{k=1}^d S(T_k;k-1,0).

Induction on the number of vertices gives

 S(T;x,y)=sigma(T)+(d+1)(A0-1)+B0.

Indeed S(T_k;k-1,0)=sigma(T_k)+k; summing k supplies the second
d(d+1)/2 and sigma(T)=(d+1)^2+sum sigma(T_k). The leaf base case is
S(leaf;x,y)=A0+B0. Subtracting it yields

 Delta S=alpha(T)+beta(T)*y/(x+1).                         (4)

At all strictly deeper states the coefficient of r_i(B) is exactly
x/(y+1) times the s(B) coefficient, because the level i profiles stay
x,y. Comparing (3) with x/(y+1) times (2), only the d+1 outer states
contribute a correction. The full r_i coefficient R is

 R=x*S/(y+1)+(d+1)A0/(y+1)
                +B0*x*[1/(y+2)-1/(y+1)].

Subtracting the leaf expression and using (4) gives

 Delta R=x*alpha(T)/(y+1)+beta(T),                        (5)

since [xy+x+y+1]/[(x+1)(y+1)]=1. Equations (4),(5) and the unchanged
lower-level ratios prove (1). This also proves that no omitted term
can depend on the full shape of B beyond the stated affine features.

LEMMA 3. PASCAL IDENTITY FOR H.

Keep all lower-level profiles fixed, and vary the terminal counts x,y.
For x,y>0 the matrix H obeys entrywise

                       H(x,y)=H(x-1,y)+H(x,y-1).          (6)

Every entry not involving the last root coordinate is a fixed constant
times L*binom(x+y,x). Entries in the last row outside its diagonal
are fixed constants times

 L*binom(x+y,x)*y/(x+1)=L*binom(x+y,x+1),

and entries in the last column outside its diagonal are fixed constants
times

 L*binom(x+y,x)*x/(y+1)=L*binom(x+y,x-1).

The last diagonal is L*binom(x+y,x). Each obeys the ordinary Pascal
identity, proving (6), including arbitrary lower-level coordinates.

If x=0,y>0, the difference H(0,y)-H(0,y-1) is supported on its last
row and diagonal. Every actual P with x=0 has r_h(P)=0, so its product
with that matrix difference vanishes. The analogous statement holds
for y=0. When x=y=0 the last feature coordinates vanish on both sides
and the remaining submatrix is precisely H for height h-1.

THEOREM PROOF.

Set C(P,Q)=f(P)^T H(u,v)f(Q) and R(P,Q)=V2(P,Q)-C(P,Q).
We prove that R is separated at each fixed profile by induction on
h+sum_i(u_i+v_i), with the empty state as base case. This integer
decreases when an external root is removed, or when an empty terminal
level is dropped. The proof is simultaneous over all possible internal
tree shapes and all lengths; no bound on either is assumed.

Suppose both terminal forests are nonempty, with final roots T,U.
Write P-=P without T, Q-=Q without U, and
delta_T=(alpha(T),0,...,0,beta(T)), delta_U analogously. The exact
reverse recurrence and deterministic excursions give

 V2(P,Q)=V2(P-,Q)+V2(P,Q-)+J(P,Q)
                     +C_T(P-,Q)+C_U(Q-,P).               (7)

Here C_U uses the symmetric, Q-active convention. By (6),

 C(P,Q)-C(P-,Q)-C(P,Q-)
 =delta_T^T H(u-e_h,v)f(Q)
                  +f(P)^T H(u,v-e_h)delta_U.             (8)

The first term in (8) is exactly expression (1) for C_T, including
each lower-level coefficient and the special last diagonal K0.
The second is exactly its symmetric counterpart for C_U. Therefore
Lemma 2 implies that each excursion minus its term of (8) equals its
leaf excursion plus a function of its active-side shape only.
The leaf excursion consists of a single state, so Lemma 1 says it
is separated in the two base shapes. J is separated by Lemma 1 too.

Subtracting (8) from (7) thus gives

 R(P,Q)=R(P-,Q)+R(P,Q-)+A(P)+B(Q),                        (9)

where A,B are some functions at the current fixed profiles; their
explicit values are irrelevant. The inductive hypothesis makes both
R terms on the right separated. Composing a one-sided function with
root deletion is still one-sided. Hence the right side, and R, are
separated.

If only one terminal forest is nonempty, there is only its deletion
branch in (7). The boundary statement of Lemma 3 makes (8) valid with
only that branch. The same argument proves separation. If both
terminal forests are empty and h>0, the reverse recurrence is the
previous-height value plus J; C restricts to its previous-height
value by Lemma 3, so separation follows by induction and Lemma 1.
For h=0 with no roots the state is empty, V2=0 and C=0.

This completes the induction, proves the separated-residual theorem,
and consequently proves exact anchor reconstruction for all lengths.
